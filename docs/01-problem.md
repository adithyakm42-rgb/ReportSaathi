# Problem Definition Document: ReportSaathi

## 1. Project Overview
ReportSaathi is an AI-assisted healthcare technology product designed to convert complex medical reports, prescriptions, and discharge summaries into plain, accessible language. It acts as a digital health companion that translates medical jargon, highlights key lab results using standard report reference ranges, clarifies dosage schedules, and helps patients prepare meaningful questions for their next doctor visit.

## 2. Target Users
- **Primary Patients:** Individuals receiving lab reports (e.g., blood tests, imaging summaries) or prescriptions who lack formal medical knowledge.
- **Family Caregivers:** Children or relatives managing health care, medication routines, and doctor appointments for elderly parents or dependents.
- **Low-Tech / Non-English Literate Patients:** Individuals in multilingual regions who prefer understanding their health information in regional or local languages.
- **Busy Healthcare Providers (Secondary Beneficiaries):** Doctors and clinic staff who benefit when patients arrive informed, focused, and prepared with clear questions.

## 3. Main User Problems
- **Medical Jargon & Abbreviations:** Complex terms like Hyperlipidemia, SGPT, eGFR, or Latin script abbreviations like t.i.d. (three times a day) confuse non-medical readers.
- **Unclear Reference Ranges:** Out-of-range lab values create panic without proper context (e.g., a slightly elevated value vs. a critical emergency).
- **Medication Confusion:** Patients leave consultations unsure of why a medicine was prescribed or how it fits into their daily schedule.
- **Time-Constrained Consultations:** Brief clinical visits leave little time for patients to ask clarifying questions or process what was said.

## 4. Existing Difficulties and Their Impact

| Existing Difficulty | Practical Example | Patient & Healthcare Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Unguided Web Searches | A patient sees "High TSH" on a report and searches Google, finding extreme stories about rare diseases. | High anxiety, unnecessary distress, or panic visits to emergency rooms. |
| Medication Non-Adherence | A patient receives a prescription reading "Metformin 500mg b.i.d." but forgets if it's before or after meals. | Missed doses, incorrect timing, or accidental double-dosing leading to side effects. |
| Language Barriers | A regional language speaker receives a discharge summary written entirely in dense clinical English. | Patient relies on informal translations from neighbors, misinterpreting instructions. |
| Unprepared Clinic Visits | A patient feels overwhelmed in front of the doctor and forgets to ask about new symptoms. | Inefficient consultations and lower quality of follow-up care. |

## 5. Proposed Solution
ReportSaathi provides a mobile-friendly or web-based interface where users upload or photograph medical documents. The system:
- **Translates Terminology:** Explains complex jargon in simple everyday terms (e.g., explaining HbA1c as "average blood sugar over the past 3 months").
- **Localizes Language:** Delivers explanations in the patient's preferred language (e.g., Hindi, Tamil, Spanish).
- **Contextualizes Lab Values:** Compares values strictly against the reference ranges printed directly on the user's specific report.
- **Clarifies Prescriptions:** Outlines purpose and schedule without altering dosage amounts.
- **Generates a Doctor Visit Prep Sheet:** Creates a concise 3–4 question checklist for the patient’s next visit.
- **Displays Red-Flag Warnings:** Prominently highlights critical out-of-range values and prompts immediate clinical attention.

## 6. Three Alternative Solution Approaches

### Approach A: Fully Automated AI Explainer (Direct-to-Consumer)
**Mechanism:** User uploads image → Optical Character Recognition (OCR) extracts text → LLM interprets content against guardrails.  
**Pros:** Instant response time, low cost per user, scalable.  
**Cons:** Higher risk of OCR extraction errors on poor-quality or handwritten documents.

### Approach B: Human-in-the-Loop Review (Hybrid Model)
**Mechanism:** AI generates an initial summary, which is verified by a trained medical assistant or nurse before being displayed to the user.  
**Pros:** Higher clinical safety and reduced error rate.  
**Cons:** Delays output delivery (30–60 minute lag) and significantly increases operational costs.

### Approach C: Clinic & Diagnostic Center Integration (B2B API)
**Mechanism:** Integrated directly into diagnostic lab software. Reports generated by labs include a ReportSaathi "Plain Language Summary" page attached at the end.  
**Pros:** Standardized data input (no OCR issues), high doctor trust.  
**Cons:** Requires long sales cycles and system integration with legacy lab IT systems.

## 7. Why ReportSaathi is a Useful Solution
ReportSaathi bridges the communication gap between diagnostic labs, doctors, and patients without disrupting existing clinical workflows.
- **Complements Doctors:** Does not attempt to treat; instead, it makes patients better prepared for their visits.
- **Empowers Patients:** Reduces panic caused by mysterious lab numbers or unguided internet searches.
- **Action-Oriented Output:** Delivers clear next steps (e.g., "Ask your doctor about X") rather than dead-end medical definitions.

## 8. Five Riskiest Assumptions
1. **OCR Accuracy:** Assuming OCR technology can reliably read low-resolution smartphone photos, poor lighting, or messy printed lab reports.
2. **Patient Trust:** Assuming patients will trust an automated digital tool to interpret their medical documents correctly.
3. **Safe Guardrail Enforcement:** Assuming LLMs will consistently adhere to system safety instructions and never give accidental diagnostic advice.
4. **Value Recognition:** Assuming patients find enough ongoing value in report explanations to use the tool regularly.
5. **Doctor Acceptance:** Assuming physicians will welcome prepared patient question lists rather than viewing them as unnecessary clinic delays.

## 9. Medical Safety Risks
- **Risk 1: Misinterpreting Critical/Urgent Results**  
  Example: Missing a severely low potassium value (K⁺ < 2.5 mmol/L) that requires immediate emergency care.  
  **Mitigation:** Implement dedicated "Red Flag Detection" algorithms that flag extreme values in bold red UI boxes instructing the user to contact emergency services immediately.
- **Risk 2: Hallucinating Medical Advice or Dosages**  
  Example: The system misreads a prescription and suggests taking 50mg instead of 5mg.  
  **Mitigation:** Strictly prohibit dosage modification or advice. System prompts must repeat: "Always take the exact dosage prescribed on your bottle."
- **Risk 3: False Sense of Reassurance**  
  Example: A patient with a "normal" blood report ignores serious physical symptoms (e.g., chest pain).  
  **Mitigation:** Display persistent legal and medical disclaimers on every screen: "Normal lab values do not rule out illness. Consult your doctor if you feel unwell."

## 10. Privacy and Legal Risks
- **Risk 1: Patient Data Breaches (PHI Exposure)**  
  **Mitigation:** Encrypt all document uploads in transit (TLS 1.3) and at rest (AES-256). Automatically strip Personally Identifiable Information (PII) such as national identification numbers or home addresses during processing.
- **Risk 2: Unlicensed Practice of Medicine**  
  **Mitigation:** Frame all outputs strictly as educational translations, not medical diagnoses or treatment plans. Ensure explicit user consent accepting terms before using the service.
- **Risk 3: Regulatory Non-Compliance (HIPAA / DISHA / GDPR)**  
  **Mitigation:** Design architecture adhering strictly to regional health data privacy standards (e.g., HIPAA in the US, DISHA/DPDP in India). Maintain short data retention schedules (e.g., delete raw images immediately after processing unless explicit consent is given).

## 11. Project Success Criteria

| Metric Category | Target Threshold | Primary Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Comprehension Gain | > 80% improvement | Measurable reduction in patient confusion |
| OCR & Extraction | > 95% parsing accuracy on printed reports | Accurate capture of lab values & ranges |
| Safety & Compliance | 0 diagnostic attempts; 0 dosage modifications | Strict adherence to safety boundaries |
| User Satisfaction | Net Promoter Score > 50 | High patient trust |
| Doctor Utility | > 70% doctors agree questions are helpful | Streamlined clinic consultation time |

## 12. What is Outside the Project Scope
To maintain product safety and focus, ReportSaathi will explicitly NOT:
- Diagnose conditions (e.g., will not state "You have Diabetes" or "You have an infection").
- Prescribe or adjust medication (will never recommend starting, stopping, increasing, or decreasing any medication or dosage).
- Invent or standardize reference ranges (will not supply external standard ranges if a lab report omits them; it only reads ranges explicitly printed on the document).
- Replace clinical consultations (will not offer direct medical opinions or act as a substitute for a licensed healthcare professional).
- Process handwritten prescriptions in V1 scope (will limit initial document processing to printed/typed reports and summaries to minimize parsing errors).
